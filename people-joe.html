Fly fishing · Ennis, Montana

Capt. Joe Dilschneider

Outfitter on the Madison River; owner of Trout Stalkers and the Madison River Fishing Company.

From St. Louis to the Madison

Joe Dilschneider grew up in St. Louis, where his father introduced him to fishing and took him on trips west. He attended St. Louis Country Day School and earned a degree in environmental studies from the University of Colorado Boulder (Madison Valley Medical Center; Madison River Fishing Company).

He guided first in Colorado and then spent ten winters guiding saltwater anglers in the Florida Keys, which is where the “Capt.” comes from (Trout Stalkers). In the winter of 1994–95 he moved to Ennis, Montana, and started Trout Stalkers. In 2016 he bought the Madison River Fishing Company, a shop on Main Street in Ennis founded in 1984 (Flylab interview).

The band, 1991

The name Trout Stalkers goes back to a chapter called “Stalking the Trout” in an old British fly-fishing book. In college Joe and a friend formed a band called Stalking Trout, which played one public gig, in 1991 at the Gold Pan Inn in Breckenridge, Colorado. Partway through the set, a stranger from the crowd asked to sit in on harmonica. It was Ian Davis, then running a fly shop in town (The Drake). See Ian’s page.

Music and the river

With bluegrass musician Arthur Hancock IV, Joe started Trout Rodeo, an annual summer bluegrass and fly-fishing festival in Ennis that raises money to put instruments into public-school classrooms. “I’ve always felt that bluegrass is the music of flyfishing,” he told The Madisonian.

He is board president of the Madison Valley Medical Center, Ennis’s community hospital, and a former director of the Fishing Outfitters Association of Montana and the Madison River Foundation (MVMC). On November 30, 2021, a gate failure at Hebgen Dam cut the flow of the upper Madison sharply, and Joe organized volunteers to rescue juvenile trout stranded in the side channels (Thomas & Thomas).
