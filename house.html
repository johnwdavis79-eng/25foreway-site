The story

Where the club came to the country

The street sits in a subdivision recorded as “New St Louis Country Club,” a name that recalls the years when the city’s oldest country club moved west to Ladue.

The St. Louis Country Club began in 1892 as a polo club. In 1895 it leased about two hundred acres near Clayton from the Davis Estate, whose lessors were the brothers Samuel C., Dwight F. and John Davis, for $400 a year. In Clayton it hired James Foulis, the 1896 U.S. Open champion, to build a nine-hole course. In 1911 the Davis Estate offered land for a new site, but the club chose ground at Ladue and Price Roads instead. Its new course, designed by Charles Blair Macdonald, opened in 1914 at 400 Barnes Road.

The Davises stayed close. Samuel C. Davis kept a summer cottage “on the Barnes road, adjoining the Country Club grounds,” and in the winter of 1928–29 he and his wife built an indoor tennis court beside it for their daughter Alita, the indoor tennis champion of the St. Louis district. The club later acquired the court, and it still carries the family name. When the club hosted the U.S. Open in 1947, its fifth hole played to a raised green Sam Davis had built.

That is the story the house sits inside: tennis, golf and a St. Louis family whose summers, and whose tennis, reached out from the Central West End to the open country of Ladue.

The neighborhood’s history

Ladue and its clubs, in brief

  1. 1892

    The St. Louis Country Club is founded as a polo club by twelve men, on Colliers Farm in Bridgeton.

  2. 1895

    The club leases about 200 acres from the Davis Estate at $400 a year. A nine-hole course by James Foulis follows in Clayton.

  3. 1911

    The club chooses a new site at Ladue and Price Roads over an offer of Davis Estate land, by 141 votes to 32 and 26.

  4. 1914

    The Macdonald course opens at 400 Barnes Road, with George Herbert Walker as golf chairman.

  5. 1925

    The club hosts the U.S. Women’s Amateur.

  6. 1929

    The Post-Dispatch reports the Davis indoor court on Barnes Road, “near Country Club grounds.”

  7. 1947

    The U.S. Open comes to Ladue. The fifth hole plays to the raised green Sam Davis built.

  8. 1959

    The club history records that it “The Board acquires an indoor tennis court from Samuel C. Davis.” The building keeps the Davis name.
